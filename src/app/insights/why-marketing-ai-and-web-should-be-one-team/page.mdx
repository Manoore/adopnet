# Why marketing, AI, and web development should be one team

Most companies assemble their growth stack the same way: a marketing agency for demand generation, a dev shop for the website, and — more recently — an AI consultant bolted on to figure out what to do with the new models everyone's talking about.

Each of those vendors does competent work in isolation. The problem shows up at the seams.

## The translation tax

Every handoff between vendors costs something. The agency writes a campaign brief that assumes a landing page structure the dev shop hasn't built yet. The AI consultant recommends a chatbot that needs data the website doesn't expose through an API. Someone has to sit in the middle translating between all three — and that someone is usually you.

We've watched this play out with founders who spent more hours in status meetings reconciling vendor deliverables than they spent on the actual strategy.

## What changes with one team

When marketing, engineering, and applied AI sit under one roof:

- A campaign idea can account for the exact page it lands on, because the same team builds both.
- An AI feature gets scoped against real product constraints, not a slide deck.
- Timelines compress, because there's no queue waiting on another vendor's sprint.

This doesn't mean generalists doing everything badly. It means specialists in each discipline who share context and ship against the same roadmap.

## The trade-off, honestly

One team means fewer opinions in the room, which is a real trade-off if you want the friction of multiple outside perspectives. For most growth-stage companies, though, the coordination cost of three vendors outweighs the benefit of three independent viewpoints — especially once you're past the exploratory phase and into execution.

If you're evaluating how to structure your own growth stack, start by counting how many meetings this quarter existed purely to sync vendors with each other. That number tells you most of what you need to know.
